# VCFO Assist passes the law, the AI flow does not yet

> **This is research, not legal advice.** It was written on 10 November 2026 from desk research and a read-only review of the firm's code. Several primary sources could not be read in full (mca.gov.in returned "Access Denied", and some statute texts came from secondary copies). A practising Company Secretary or an advocate must read this report, check the sources and confirm each verdict in writing **before any live use** of VCFO Assist on the MCA portal and **before Ask VCFO handles real client data**. Under SBC's own rule, anything unclear counts as prohibited until that written opinion exists.

**Bottom line.** VCFO Assist, as designed, looks lawful. It is a staff-only Chrome extension that fills SPICe+ fields in the staff member's own logged-in session. It never touches login, captcha, OTP, DSC signing, final Submit or payment, and it reads the SRN off the screen. MCA's published site terms do not ban browser extensions or form-filling tools. Their only rule on automation is about heavy, high-volume database access ([MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)). The IT Act punishes access "without permission", and a registered user working in their own session has MCA's permission ([India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)). So most of the extension's acts are **Allowed with conditions**, not Prohibited. The firm's real legal exposure sits in two other places. The first is the **certifying professional's signature**. ICAI has suspended a CA for 120 days for putting dummy phone numbers into mandatory SPICe+ fields "to bypass system checks" ([ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)). A fast tool makes that kind of mistake easier to repeat. The second is **Ask VCFO**. In production today it sends prompts to Anthropic's API with no India residency setting. Only identity numbers (PAN, Aadhaar, passport, DIN) are masked; names, addresses, emails and phones are not. Without the client's written consent, sending client information to a third party is already professional misconduct for a CA ([AdvocateKhoj, CA Act Second Schedule](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/charteredaccountants/schedule2part1.php)). Most DPDP duties, with penalties up to ₹250 crore, start around **13 May 2027** ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [DPDPA.com, Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html)). The labels-only field resolver sends no personal data and is low risk on any provider. The fix for Ask VCFO is clear: get client consent and a processor contract now, then move real-data calls to Amazon Bedrock's India-only profile. The firm's current Bedrock code would not stay in India as written.

## MCA's own terms do not ban attended filling tools

The earlier report could not read MCA's terms of use. That gap is now closed, with one caveat. The full "Website Policies" text was read from an official NIC-hosted MCA copy dated 2018 ([MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)). It binds anyone who browses the site. It forbids "unauthorized use", "unauthorized entry into mca.gov.in" and "misuse of passwords". Its only clause on automation is aimed at load. MCA may cut off users who generate "unusually high numbers of daily database accesses, whether generated manually or in an automated fashion". The text has **no clause on browser extensions, bots or third-party software**. The GST portal is different. Its user agreement allows access only "through the interfaces expressly authorised by GSTN" and calls scraping an offence under IT Act s.43 ([TaxHeal, GST portal notice](https://www.taxheal.com/scrapping-of-gst-portal-site-is-strictly-prohibited-gstn.html)). MCA has no such clause. The caveat matters: the 2018 text still mentions the 1956 Act, and the live V3 pages could not be opened. If the live V3 terms add an "authorised interfaces only" or "no automated tools" clause, every Allowed-with-conditions row below drops to Needs legal opinion. A CS must open the live terms from an Indian network and confirm.

The IT Act points the same way. Section 43 makes access, downloading or extraction "without permission of the owner" a civil wrong, and s.66 makes it a crime (up to 3 years or ₹5 lakh) only when done dishonestly or fraudulently ([India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)). "Access" includes "instructing or communicating with" a computer. MCA gives its registered Business User permission to log in, type, save and download their own form. An extension that types on that user's instruction, inside that session, is the user's tool. No Indian court has ruled on this exact pattern, so this is reasoned from the statute, not settled. Section 66C (identity theft, up to 3 years and ₹1 lakh) applies only to use of another person's password, signature or other identity feature. VCFO Assist never touches any of those. No notification declaring MCA21 a "protected system" under s.70 was found. Such a notification would limit access to authorised persons, with up to 10 years' jail for others ([India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content); [Legally India](https://www.legallyindia.com/blogs/protecting-critical-information-infrastructures-in-india/)). The Gazette could not be searched, so this is "not found", not "confirmed absent".

The market shows tolerance, not permission. RMK Tech Services sells a Chrome extension that even fills MCA V3 user IDs and passwords, leaving captcha, OTP and the click to the person ([RMK Tech Services](https://rmktechservices.com/portal-login-filler/mca-login)). SAG Infotech advertises V3 pre-filling ([SAG Infotech blog](https://blog.saginfotech.com/roc-software-handle-mca-ver-3-forms-filing)). No MCA objection to any of them was found. VCFO Assist is more careful than RMK, because it never touches credentials. The closest court ruling is the Supreme Court's IRCTC case of January 2025. It allowed prosecution of an unauthorised person using hundreds of fake user IDs to sell railway tickets, under a Railways Act offence ([Supreme Court judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2017/6192/6192_2017_15_1501_58348_Judgement_09-Jan-2025.pdf)). The Companies Act has no matching offence. The lesson is the line courts draw: fake or shared IDs and getting around portal controls.

## The certifier's signature is where firms actually get caught

No enforcement case found anywhere turns on the software used to type a form. Every one turns on what the professional certified. The SPICe+ (INC-32) certificate says the professional "verified the above particulars (including attachment(s)) from the original/certified records ... and found them to be true, correct and complete" ([ICAI DC/1890/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/85.-DC-1890-2024.pdf)). It also says the professional "personally visited the premises of the proposed registered office" ([ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)). In February 2026 ICAI removed CA Muni Kumar Gubiligari from the register for **120 days** and fined him **₹20,000**. He had entered dummy landline numbers to get past mandatory fields and relied on documents sent by email and WhatsApp. ICAI said his negligence "provided a platform for the preparators to misuse the system". In January 2025 CA Rounak Kumar Bansal was **reprimanded** for declaring a site visit and in-person signing that never happened, after receiving papers through another CA ([ICAI DC/1720/2023](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/130.-DC-1720-2023.pdf)). CA Puneet Jain was reprimanded even though he said his DSC was forged, because "responsibility for safekeeping a digital signature lies with its owner" ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/ca-reprimanded-misuse-dsc-mca-filing.html)). ICSI applies the same due-diligence clause to company secretaries ([ICSI, CSJ July 2025](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/July-2025/36.pdf)).

Evidence wins these cases. A CA was cleared when MCA's own team showed the filings came from a different IP address than his ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/icai-disciplinary-committee-clears-ca-from-professional-misconduct-for-alleged-connivance-in-incorporation-of-shell-companies-read-order/345145)). Others were cleared because they kept written consent from the premises owner or proof of their checks ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/icai-slams-5-year-late-roc-inspection-clears-ca-in-alleged-false-certification-case-397385.html)). So the `assist_runs` audit trail is an evidence asset. It must also capture the certifier's own checks, not just the fills. The scale of risk is real: the government recommended action against about **400 CAs and CSs** over Chinese-linked shell companies in 2022 ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/disciplinary-action-initiated-against-400-chartered-accountants-company-secretaries-for-incorporating-chinese-shell-companies/185225)).

The penalty ladder runs from small to severe. A ROC fined a company and a director **₹10,000 each** in February 2026 for an AOC-4 filed with wrong data, with no fraud alleged ([ICSI, ROC adjudication orders](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/Mar2026/34.pdf)). Institute discipline brings reprimands, fines (one ICAI fine was **₹2 lakh** for a due-diligence lapse on an MCA filing ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/icai-levies-rs-2-lakh-penalty-on-ca-for-lapse-in-due-diligence-on-mca-filing-held-guilty-for-professional-misconduct-404467.html))) or removal for a period. At the top, a false statement in a certificate (s.448) is punished as fraud under s.447: **6 months to 10 years** in jail and a fine from the amount involved up to **three times** it, where the fraud is at least ₹10 lakh or 1% of turnover ([ca2013.com, s.447](https://ca2013.com/447-punishment-for-fraud/)). Fraud needs "intent to deceive". In January 2026 the Supreme Court held that a s.448 case must run through s.447 and the s.212(6) bar, and cannot start on a private complaint ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/cognizance-under-section-448-requires-section-447-sc)). So an honest tool error is unlikely to be criminal. But misconduct needs only a lack of due diligence, and a wrong value copied silently into many forms looks like a pattern, not a slip.

## The AI data flow: today's setup sends client data to the US

Two different AI features exist, and they carry very different risk.

**The VCFO Assist field resolver is low risk.** When the portal changes a form, the resolver sends one field label and up to 40 candidate labels to Claude. It sends no values. Before any call, it refuses (rather than masks) any payload that looks like a PAN, Aadhaar, passport, DIN, email, mobile number or 8-digit run. The model can only return an index from the list (`src/lib/assist-agents/resolve.ts`, `src/lib/assist-agents/pii.ts`). Because no personal data leaves, no DPDP processor contract or cross-border issue arises. That holds only while the labels-only guard (G9) holds. Anthropic's Usage Policy does not treat label matching as a high-risk use.

**Ask VCFO is the real exposure.** The code review found these facts:

**Is it legal to send real client data to Anthropic today?** DPDP does not forbid it. Section 16 allows transfers abroad unless the government restricts a country, and no restricted list exists ([DPDPA.com, Rule 15](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule15.html)). Section 16 itself starts only around 13 May 2027. But three rules bite now. First, a CA who discloses engagement information "to any person other than his client ... without the consent of his client" commits misconduct under Clause (1), Part I, Second Schedule of the CA Act ([AdvocateKhoj](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/charteredaccountants/schedule2part1.php)). Hosting with AWS is generally seen as using a tool. A chat that sends a client's facts to an AI provider is closer to disclosure. The UK's ICAEW says entering client data into public AI tools "will likely breach confidentiality" ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2024-02/dont-let-ai-tools-land-you-in-hot-water)). Second, the SPDI Rules are in force until about May 2027. Their transfer rule (Rule 7) needs consent or contractual necessity plus equal protection for sensitive data such as bank details ([KSK, SPDI after DPDP](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/)). The exact Rule 7 text was not fetched. Third, Anthropic's Usage Policy, effective 12 November 2026, treats legal advice and advice on an individual's own taxes as **high-risk**. A qualified person must review the output before it reaches the person, and the person must be told AI was involved ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)).

**Bedrock India is the better route, but not a full cure.** It removes the cross-border question and takes Anthropic out of the data path. It does not remove the CA confidentiality duty or the DPDP consent duty. Client consent and a processor contract are needed on either route.

## What each law requires, when it starts, and what it costs

SBC is a "body corporate" under the IT Act, because the definition covers "a firm, sole proprietorship or other association of individuals engaged in commercial or professional activities" ([CERT-In FAQs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf)). Under DPDP it is a **Data Fiduciary** in its own right, because it decides its own tools, retention and AI features. AWS and Anthropic are its **Data Processors**. DPDP has **no "contract" or "legitimate interests" ground**. Processing needs notice and consent, or a listed legitimate use. The "voluntarily provided" use (s.7(a)) fits a director who uploads data personally. It is hard to stretch to data passed on by the client company ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/interpreting-voluntarily-provided-section7a-dpdp-act-524783)). So the safe course is a short notice and consent from each director.

Rule 6 safeguards: encryption or masking, access control, logs and monitoring, backups. Keep data and logs at least **one year** (Rule 8(3)). Breach notice to the Board immediately and in detail within **72 hours**, and to each affected person "without delay" (Rule 7) ([DPDPA.com, Rule 6](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html); [Rule 7](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule7.html); [Rule 8](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule8.html)). | Mostly from **~13 May 2027** (some say 12 May). Second, add a guard that never fills a value missing from source data, after Gubiligari. Fourth, G14's "CERT-In 180 days" should become "at least one year", to meet DPDP Rule 8(3) as well. ## Conclusion

The research turns the firm's worry around. The extension, which looked like the legal risk, is the most careful tool found in the market. Its guards keep it on the right side of every line the law draws. The feature that looked routine, an AI chat inside the firm's own app, is the one already exposed today. It is exposed not by any new data law, but by the oldest rule in the profession: do not share a client's affairs without the client's consent. That makes the next six months a sequencing problem rather than a legal block. Client consent and the CERT-In runbook are live duties and come first. Bedrock India and director notices follow, before DPDP's May 2027 start.

A second lesson is about speed. Every disciplinary order found punishes a professional who signed something untrue. A tool that fills forms in seconds raises the volume of filings without raising the certifier's diligence. So the most valuable control SBC can build is not another refusal in the extension. It is a gate in Suite that will not show the Submit card until the certifier's own evidence is attached. That same record is what wins a hearing if one ever comes. None of this replaces the written CS opinion. The opinion is now an easier question to answer, because most of the facts it needs are in this report.
