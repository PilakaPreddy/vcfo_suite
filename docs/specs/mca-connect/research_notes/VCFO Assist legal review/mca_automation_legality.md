# Legality of attended browser auto-fill of MCA21 V3 (SPICe+) by a firm's own Chrome extension (VCFO Assist), India, as of 10 November 2026

Scope note. This is desk research, not legal advice. It builds on the firm's earlier notes in `research_notes/MCA SPICe filing automation options/legal_limits.md` and the report `reports/MCA SPICe filing automation options.md`. Points those notes already established (CCA DSC custody, DPDP processor contracts, the 2021 to 2023 shell-company enforcement wave, ICAI lien and DSC-misuse orders) are not repeated except where this pass confirms or corrects them.

Research limit. mca.gov.in (including its PDFs, robots.txt and the Wayback Machine copies) returned HTTP 403 "Access Denied" from its Akamai edge in this session, and web.archive.org was blocked by the session's egress policy. The MCA "Website Policies" text below was therefore read in full from an official NIC-hosted copy (mcacdm.nic.in, an MCA domain), not from mca.gov.in itself. The IT Act text was read in full from India Code. The Companies Act s.447 text was read from a compiled copy (ca2013.com), not India Code.

What is new in this pass, in one list:
1. The full MCA "Website Policies" / terms of use text was found and read. It has no clause on browser extensions, bots or third-party software. It has an acceptable-use clause aimed at bulk or high-volume database access "whether generated manually or in an automated fashion", and a clause that prohibits "unauthorized use", "unauthorized entry into mca.gov.in" and "misuse of passwords".
2. Contrast: the GST portal's user agreement expressly limits access to "interfaces expressly authorised by GSTN" and names web scraping as an unauthorised activity under IT Act s.43. No equivalent MCA clause was found.
3. IT Act text confirmed from India Code, including the statutory definition of "access" (which covers "instructing or communicating with" a computer) and the 2023 Jan Vishwas change that made s.72A a civil penalty of up to ₹25 lakh (this resolves the conflict the earlier notes flagged).
4. No s.70 "protected system" notification for MCA21 was found; known notifications cover other systems (UIDAI CIDR, Income Tax Systems, Census/NPR, some banks).
5. New ICAI orders (February 2026) reproduce the exact INC-32 professional certification text and show a CA removed from the register for 120 days partly for putting fake phone numbers in mandatory fields "to bypass system checks". This is the single most relevant precedent for an auto-fill tool.
6. New Supreme Court authority (January 2026, Yerram Vijay Kumar) on how s.448 prosecutions must run through s.447 and the s.212(6) bar.
7. Market precedent: a commercial Chrome extension (RMK Tech Services) already fills MCA V3 user ID and password and leaves captcha, OTP and the click to the human; SAG Infotech and ComplyRelax advertise V3 pre-fill. No MCA objection or enforcement against any of them was found.
8. Supreme Court IRCTC judgment (January 2025) is the closest Indian authority on automated or unauthorised use of a government booking portal, but it rests on a specific Railways Act offence that has no Companies Act equivalent.

Classification key used below: **Allowed**, **Allowed with conditions**, **Needs legal opinion**, **Prohibited**. Under SBC's own rule (unclear = prohibited; no tool on the live portal before written CS approval), anything below "Allowed" still needs the firm's written CS sign-off before staff use it.

## 1. What MCA's own terms, policies, FAQs and circulars say about third-party software, extensions, bots, automated access and scraping

### Takeaway
MCA's published site terms neither permit nor forbid browser extensions or form-filling software. They bind every visitor by browsing ("By accessing this site ... you agree to be bound"), prohibit unauthorised use, unauthorised entry and misuse of passwords, and reserve the right to cut off anyone who degrades service with "unusually high numbers of daily database accesses, whether generated manually or in an automated fashion". An attended extension that fills fields inside a user's own session, at human speed, is not caught by any of that text. Unlike GSTN, MCA has not said its portal may be used only through MCA-authorised interfaces.

### Cited Findings
- Full text of MCA "Website Policies" (official copy on mcacdm.nic.in, PDF created 11 January 2018). Opening notice: "Please read these terms and conditions carefully. By accessing this site and any pages thereof, you agree to be bound by the terms and conditions below. If you do not agree to the terms and conditions below, do not access this site, or any pages thereof." — [MCA, Website Policies (mcacdm.nic.in copy)](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)
- Same document, "Terms of Usage", the only clause that mentions automation: "Acceptable use of MCA Searchable Databases: MCA searchable database is designed to meet the needs of a wide range of users wishing to interrogate our information on-line. Due to limitations of equipment and bandwidth, they are not intended to be a source for bulk downloads. Individuals, companies, IP addresses or blocks of IP addresses who deny or degrade service to other users by generating unusually high numbers of daily database accesses, whether generated manually or in an automated fashion, may be denied access to these services without notice" — [MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)
- Same document, "Use of information and materials": "Unauthorized use of this web site and system including but not limited to unauthorized entry into mca.gov.in, misuse of passwords, or misuse of any information posted on this site is strictly prohibited and would attract both penal and punitive action." — [MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)
- Same document: "Certain sections or pages on this site may contain separate terms and conditions, which are in addition to these terms and conditions. In the event of a conflict, the additional terms and conditions of MCA will govern for those sections or pages." Governing law: "Use of this site shall be governed by of India [sic] and the courts of New Delhi shall have exclusive jurisdiction". — [MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)
- Same document, privacy: the site logs the user's IP address; registered users' information is held on a secure server; "MCA may use information it holds to prevent and detect fraud" and may share it with other government organisations or law enforcement for that purpose. — [MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf)
- The same terms appear to be published on mca.gov.in as `Website_Policies.pdf` (search extract begins "NOTICE. Please read these terms and conditions carefully"), but that copy returned 403 and could not be compared line by line. — [MCA, Website_Policies.pdf (search result)](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca/documents/Website_Policies.pdf)
- MCA's V3 hyperlinking and copyright policies (mobile-app policy pages) state contents are "the sole and exclusive property" of MCA, encourage links to the site, and require prior permission to hyperlink to the app; they say nothing about automation. — [MCA, Hyperlinking Policy](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/home/mca-mobile-app-policies/hyperlinking-policy.html); [MCA, Copyright Policy](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/home/mca-mobile-app-policies/copyright-policy.html) (read via search extract only)
- Comparator: the GST portal's user agreement says services "shall be accessed only through the interfaces expressly authorised by GSTN", defines "Unauthorised activities" to include "any activity which is punishable under section 43 (for eg web scraping ...)", and a 5 December 2018 GST portal notice said "Scrapping of GST Site is strictly prohibited". — [TaxHeal, reproducing GST portal notice of 05.12.2018](https://www.taxheal.com/scrapping-of-gst-portal-site-is-strictly-prohibited-gstn.html) (aggregator; verify on gst.gov.in)
- MCA V3 FAQs and the ICSI-hosted V3 login FAQ (already in the earlier notes) tie one DSC to one user ID and tell users not to share OTP or password; no FAQ found addresses third-party software. — [MCA, V3 FAQs 20-02-2022](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca/pdf/v3FAQs_20022022.pdf); [ICSI, MCA-21 V3 Login FAQs](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/FAQs_MCA-21_Login_Registration.pdf)
- An older ICSI explainer on MCA21 describes the original model as downloading the blank e-form and filling it offline with freely available software, with citizens able to file directly or through a facilitation office; this shows MCA historically expected users to prepare forms with their own tools. — [ICSI, MCA 21 page](https://www.icsi.edu/member/miscellaneous/mca-21) (old background)
- No MCA circular, FAQ or advisory on third-party filing software, browser extensions or bots was found in searches; the only automation-related MCA communications found are anti-phishing advisories and portal-glitch notices. — [WebSearch results summarised in this note; see MCA on X re third-party payment vendor](https://x.com/MCA21India/status/1798650242835083392)

### Inferences
- The binding MCA text is a "browsewrap" accepted by use. Its prohibitions turn on "unauthorized" use or entry and "misuse of passwords". A registered Business User who logs in personally and uses a tool to type into fields of their own form is an authorised user doing an authorised act; the tool does not enter the system on its own account.
- The only automation clause is about load ("unusually high numbers of daily database accesses") and bulk download from the searchable databases. Its stated remedy is denial of access without notice, not a penalty. VCFO Assist's acts (one form at a time, human-paced) are far from this, but any loop, retry storm or company-search harvesting would come within it.
- MCA's terms lack the GSTN "authorised interfaces only" clause. That clause is what turns tool use into a contractual breach on the GST portal; its absence on MCA is a meaningful (though not conclusive) point in VCFO Assist's favour.
- The terms text read here dates from 2018 and still refers to the Companies Act 1956, so it was carried over from V2. V3 pages may carry "separate terms" that govern on conflict; none were found, but that cannot be ruled out without reading mca.gov.in directly.

### Gaps
- The live mca.gov.in V3 terms page, any V3 Business User registration terms shown at sign-up, and any terms shown inside the SPICe+ web form could not be read (403). A CS should open them from an Indian network and confirm that they match the mcacdm.nic.in copy and contain no extension, bot or "authorised interface" clause.
- No MCA circular search was possible on mca.gov.in itself.

## 2. Companies Act 2013 (s.7(1)(b), Rule 14/INC-8, s.447, s.448): liability of the certifying professional when a tool fills the data

### Takeaway
Nothing in the Companies Act or Incorporation Rules regulates how a form is typed, so pre-filling by software is not itself an offence or a breach. What the law regulates is the truth of the particulars and the personal certification. The INC-32 certificate says the professional "verified the above particulars (including attachment(s)) from the original/certified records", and the ICAI punishes certifiers for wrong data whoever keyed it, including for placeholder values used to get past mandatory fields. So a tool is lawful only if every value it fills is traceable to verified source records and the certifier actually checks the filled form before signing.

### Cited Findings
- Exact INC-32 (SPICe+) certification text, as reproduced in an ICAI Disciplinary Committee order: "I have been duly engaged for the purpose of certification of this form. It is hereby also certified that I have gone through the provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 and rules thereunder for the subject matter of this form and matters incidental thereto and I have verified the above particulars (including attachment(s)) from the original/certified records maintained by the applicant which is subject matter of this form and found them to be true, correct and complete and no information material to this form has been suppressed." — [ICAI DC/1890/2024, ROC Karnataka v. CA Ritesh Upadhyay](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/85.-DC-1890-2024.pdf)
- INC-32 also contains the declaration: "I further declare that I have personally visited the premises of the proposed registered Office given in the form at the address mentioned herein above and verified that the said registered office of the Company will be functioning for the business purposes of the Company". — [ICAI DC/1891/2024, ROC Karnataka v. CA Muni Kumar Gubiligari, order 11 February 2026](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)
- In DC/1891/2024 the CA "entered fake phone numbers like 080-10000000/ 080-40000000 in incorporation forms to bypass system checks ... These numbers appeared to be imaginary and were used to meet the requirement of entering the landline number in the mandatory field"; he was held guilty under Item (7), Part I, Second Schedule (due diligence), along with failures to verify originals (he "relied upon the copy of documents produced before him through email/ Whatsapp"), Rule 13 witnessing, director consent and registered-office visits. Punishment: removal from the register for 120 days and a ₹20,000 fine. The Committee said the negligence "provided a platform for the preparators to misuse the system". — [ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)
- In the same order the charge that the CA "provided his firms' e-mail as the email id of the Company in incorporation documents" was held **Not Guilty**, as was a charge that the email in MCA records differed from the company letterhead. — [ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)
- In DC/1890/2024 (INC-32 with a DIR-2 attachment whose signatures were alleged to be pasted), the CA was reprimanded and fined ₹5,000 (lenient view on request). — [ICAI DC/1890/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/85.-DC-1890-2024.pdf)
- Other ICAI orders: a certifier who "relied upon the verification done by the other professional" and certified INC-32 without personally verifying the registered office was held at fault; a CA who signed SPICe documents without physically visiting the registered office was held guilty of gross negligence. — [ICAI disciplinary orders listing (search extract)](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/162.-DC-1628-2022.pdf); [StudyCafe, CA signing SPICe documents without physical verification guilty](https://studycafe.in/ca-signing-spice-documents-without-making-physical-verification-guilty-of-professional-misconduct-312909.html)
- s.447 (compiled text valid to 26 August 2026): fraud of at least ₹10 lakh or 1% of turnover (whichever is lower) carries imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years and a fine from the amount involved up to three times it; at least 3 years where public interest is involved; below the threshold and without public interest, up to 5 years or a fine up to ₹20 lakh or both. "Fraud" requires an act, omission or concealment "with intent to deceive", whether or not there is wrongful gain or loss. — [ca2013.com, s.447](https://ca2013.com/447-punishment-for-fraud/) (confirms the figures the earlier notes left unverified)
- Supreme Court, Yerram Vijay Kumar v. State of Telangana (reported 14 January 2026): cognisance of an offence under s.448 must invoke s.447 and comply with the second proviso to s.212(6); "cognisance cannot be taken merely by the filing of a private complaint"; proceedings under ss.448 and 451 were quashed. — [Taxmann summary](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/cognizance-under-section-448-requires-section-447-sc); [Inshorts](https://inshorts.com/en/news/s--448-companies-act-must-be-read-along-with-s--447--supreme-court-1768048837188)
- s.7(5), s.7(6) and s.448 text and the Rule 14/INC-8 professional declaration are as stated in the earlier notes. — [TaxGuru, Section 7](https://taxguru.in/?p=66795); [LegalMantra, s.448](https://legalmantra.net/blog-detail/Section-448-of-The-Companies-Act-2013); [ca2013.com, Rule 14](https://ca2013.com/rule-14-companies-incorporation-rules-2014/)
- The Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 was introduced on 23 March 2026 and a JPC reported on 3 August 2026; no source found shows it changes s.7, s.447 or s.448 or who may certify SPICe+. — [Corporate Professionals (19 June 2026)](https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/?p=11436); [LawSikho explainer](https://lawsikho.com/blog/?p=4990)

### Inferences
- **Pre-filling by software is not itself an issue.** The certificate is a statement about the truth of particulars and the professional's verification, not about who keyed them. Liability (s.7(5)/(6), s.448 via s.447, and Item (7) misconduct) attaches to the person who certifies or signs, regardless of the tool.
- Criminal exposure under s.447 needs "intent to deceive", and the 2026 Supreme Court ruling channels s.448 prosecutions through s.447 and the s.212(6) bar, so an honest data-entry error by a tool is unlikely to be criminal. But disciplinary exposure under Item (7) needs only a lack of due diligence, and that is where an auto-fill tool raises the stakes: a wrong value copied silently into many forms is a pattern, not a slip.
- The Gubiligari order makes one design rule non-negotiable: VCFO Assist must never fill a placeholder, dummy or "make it pass" value into a mandatory field. If source data is missing, it must leave the field empty and stop.
- The order's not-guilty finding on the firm's email address is reassuring but narrow. It is safer for the tool to fill the company's own contact details, and to show the certifier which fields carry firm-side values.
- The verification evidence the certifier needs is the same with or without the tool: originals or certified copies, registered-office visit, Rule 13 witnessing, director consents. VCFO Assist should record, per form, the source of each filled value, who reviewed the filled form and when, so that the certifier can show the Committee independent verification.

### Gaps
- The Companies (Incorporation) Rules text on SPICe+ Part B attachments and the current V3 web-form certification text were not read on mca.gov.in; the wording above comes from ICAI orders that quote forms filed in 2021 to 2023 (V2 era). The V3 web form may word it differently.
- No ICSI order found that is specifically about SPICe+ data errors in 2025 or 2026 (one July 2025 ICSI case concerns INC-20A due diligence).

## 3. IT Act 2000: s.43, s.66, s.66C, s.70, s.72A and what "permission" means for attended filling in one's own session

### Takeaway
s.43 and s.66 turn on access or data extraction "without permission of the owner or any other person who is in charge" of the computer. A registered MCA Business User has MCA's permission to log in, enter and save form data, download their own form and read their own SRN; an extension acting on that user's instructions inside that session is the user's tool, not a separate intruder, and no Indian authority treats it otherwise. s.66C is not engaged because the extension never uses anyone's password, signature or other identification feature. No s.70 protected-system notification for MCA21 was found. s.72A is now a civil penalty of up to ₹25 lakh.

### Cited Findings
- s.2(1)(a): "access" "means gaining entry into, instructing or communicating with the logical, arithmetical, or memory function resources of a computer, computer system or computer network". — [India Code, IT Act 2000 (updated text)](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- s.43: "If any person without permission of the owner or any other person who is in charge of a computer, computer system or computer network,– (a) accesses or secures access to such computer ...; (b) downloads, copies or extracts any data, computer data base or information ...; ... (e) disrupts or causes disruption ...; ... (g) provides any assistance to any person to facilitate access ... in contravention of the provisions of this Act ...; ... (i) destroys, deletes or alters any information residing in a computer resource ..." he is "liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the person so affected". — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- s.66: "If any person, dishonestly or fraudulently, does any act referred to in section 43, he shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years or with fine which may extend to five lakh rupees or with both", with "dishonestly" and "fraudulently" taking their IPC meanings. — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- s.66C: "Whoever, fraudulently or dishonestly make use of the electronic signature, password or any other unique identification feature of any other person, shall be punished with imprisonment ... which may extend to three years and shall also be liable to fine which may extend to rupees one lakh." — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- s.70: the appropriate Government "may, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare any computer resource which directly or indirectly affects the facility of Critical Information Infrastructure, to be a protected system"; it "may, by order in writing, authorise the persons who are authorised to access protected systems"; unauthorised access or attempt is punishable with up to ten years and fine. — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- Known s.70 notifications found: UIDAI CIDR (21 December 2015), TETRA network (2010), Directorate of Income Tax (Systems) resources, Census/NPR systems (RGI, 7 November 2022), ICICI Bank IT resources (16 June 2020); none for MCA or MCA21 was found. — [Legally India](https://www.legallyindia.com/blogs/protecting-critical-information-infrastructures-in-india/); [cacult, Income Tax protected systems](https://cacult.com/designation-of-protected-systems-for-income-tax-department/); [Cyber Secure India, ICICI](https://cybersecureindia.in/government-india-meity-declares-icici-banks-resources-critical-information-infrastructure/); [ApniLaw, s.70](https://www.apnilaw.com/legal-articles/acts/section-70-of-the-it-act-2000-all-about-infrastructure-protection/)
- s.72A now reads "[Penalty] for disclosure of information in breach of lawful contract", and a person who, while providing services under a lawful contract, discloses personal information without consent or in breach of contract with intent to cause, or knowing it likely to cause, wrongful loss or gain "shall be liable to penalty which may extend to twenty-five lakh rupees" (substituted by the Jan Vishwas Act, Act 18 of 2023, w.e.f. 30 November 2023). — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content) (resolves the conflict in the earlier notes: it is no longer an imprisonment offence)
- s.45 residuary penalty for contravening rules, regulations, directions or orders under the Act: up to ₹1 lakh plus compensation up to ₹10 lakh (company) or ₹1 lakh (other person). — [India Code, IT Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content)
- Commentators: India has no scraping statute; s.43 arguments gain force when a tool gets past logins, defeats access controls or degrades a site. — [Ikigai Law](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/263/legality-of-data-scraping-in-india); [LawRato](https://lawrato.com/indian-kanoon/civil-law/is-web-scraping-legal-in-india-what-a-business-can-and-cannot-collect-3192)
- Allahabad HC (September 2026) read s.66C narrowly as covering only use of the named credentials. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/allahabad-high-court/allahabad-hc-email-id-another-name-prima-facie-identity-theft-66c-it-act-548769) (from earlier notes)

### Inferences
- "Permission" under s.43 is given by the owner or person in charge, here MCA. MCA's grant is to the registered user, for filing their forms. When that user, logged in, tells an extension to type values into their own form and press Save, the "access" (instructing the computer) is still the user's, within the permission given. The Website Policies prohibit "unauthorized" use and entry; they do not withdraw permission for tool-assisted input. On the evidence found, s.43(a) and (b) are not engaged by VCFO Assist's listed acts.
- The facts that would change this: getting past the captcha, OTP or login (an access control); acting in someone else's session or under someone else's user ID; hitting MCA endpoints directly; loading the system heavily (s.43(e) disruption, and the terms' denial-of-access clause); or extracting data beyond the user's own filings (s.43(b)). VCFO Assist's design excludes all of these, and the firm should keep them excluded in code, not only in policy.
- s.66 needs dishonesty or fraud on top of a s.43 act, so it is remote for honest, attended filling.
- s.66C: VCFO Assist never stores or types anyone's password, OTP or DSC PIN and never signs. The risk is not the extension but the surrounding practice: staff logging in with a client's or director's user ID, or a signer's token being operated by staff. Those are already flagged prohibited in the earlier notes.
- s.70: if MCA21 were ever notified as a protected system, only persons authorised in writing under s.70(2) could access it and the penalty would be up to 10 years. Registered users would presumably be authorised by the notification's own terms, but a tool's status would then need counsel review. No notification was found, so this is a watch item, not a current bar.
- s.72A: client data flowing from the practice app into the browser is disclosed only to MCA (the purpose of the engagement), so s.72A is not engaged by filling; it matters for where the extension sends or stores data (it should send nothing to third parties).

### Gaps
- egazette.gov.in could not be searched (TLS failure from this session), so the absence of an MCA21 s.70 notification is "not found", not "confirmed absent".
- No Indian judgment was found that interprets "permission" in s.43 for a tool used by an authorised user inside their own session.

## 4. ICSI and ICAI professional conduct: due diligence in certifying e-forms, DSC and login use, and guidance on automation or AI tools

### Takeaway
Both institutes judge the certifier on verification, not on tooling. No ICSI or ICAI guidance note, code clause or circular on automation, RPA, browser extensions or AI tools in filing practice was found as of November 2026; ICSI has only published journal articles on AI governance and ICAI has an AI Committee that runs training. The firm should therefore apply the general due-diligence and confidentiality duties to the tool.

### Cited Findings
- Item (7), Part I, Second Schedule, Chartered Accountants Act: a CA in practice is guilty of professional misconduct if he "does not exercise due diligence, or is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties"; ICAI applies it to SPICe+/INC-32 certification. — [ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf)
- ICSI (Chartered Secretary, July 2025): a practising CS was held guilty under Clause (7), Part I, Second Schedule (same due-diligence clause in the Company Secretaries Act) and reprimanded and fined; "Company Secretaries in Practice are expected to exercise due diligence while certifying/ filing form INC-20A along with all mandatory documents". — [ICSI, CSJ July 2025](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/July-2025/36.pdf)
- ICSI (Chartered Secretary, September 2024), "Filing of Forms - Professional Misconduct": misconduct under the clause is discussed as requiring gross or habitual negligence; the committee rejected a respondent's reliance on service-rule case law. — [ICSI, CSJ September 2024](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/September-2024/29.pdf)
- ICSI (CSJ July 2024): a CS has a duty "to prevent any unauthorized use of his or her Digital Signatures/DSC". — [ICSI, CSJ July 2024](https://icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/July-2024/35.pdf) (from earlier notes)
- ICSI journal (October 2025) on AI for governance: company secretaries should lead ethical oversight of AI deployments and set AI ethics policies; this is commentary, not a member rule. — [ICSI, CSJ October 2025](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/October_2025/18.pdf)
- ICAI has an AI Committee that runs webinars on AI use (for example in tax audit); no binding member guidance on AI or automation tools was found. — [ICAI, AI Committee](https://www.icai.org/category/ai-committee)
- Comparator only (not Indian): UK professional bodies' January 2026 guidance says members "remain ultimately accountable" for AI-assisted work and that putting client data into public AI tools is likely a confidentiality breach without consent. — [ICAS et al., PCRT AI topical guidance, 19 January 2026](https://icas-com.uksouth01.umbraco.io/media/g2lbxwop/pcrt-ai-topical-guidance-19012026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The due-diligence clause is the real control on VCFO Assist. A certifier who signs a tool-filled form without reading it against source records is in the same position as the Gubiligari or "relied on another professional's verification" respondents.
- Confidentiality duties under both Acts' Schedules, and DPDP, mean the extension must keep client data between the practice app and mca.gov.in; any AI-assisted field mapping should not send client data to an external model without a processor contract and client notice (see earlier DPDP notes).

### Gaps
- No ICSI or ICAI text specific to automation, extensions, RPA or AI in statutory filing was found; icsi.edu and icai.org announcements could not be searched comprehensively.
- ICMAI (cost accountants) and Bar Council (advocates, who may also give the INC-8 declaration) were not checked.

## 5. Market precedents and any MCA objection or enforcement; MCA anti-bot measures (captcha, OTP, V3 MFA)

### Takeaway
Tool-assisted MCA work is common and openly sold. A Chrome extension already fills MCA V3 login credentials while leaving captcha, OTP and the click to the human; SAG Infotech (Gen CompLaw) and ComplyRelax advertise V3 pre-fill. No MCA objection, warning or enforcement against any of them was found. V3 login uses password plus captcha plus OTP (or DSC login); no MCA notice on new bot controls was found, and late-2026 reporting is about portal glitches, not crackdowns.

### Cited Findings
- RMK Tech Services (Ballari, Karnataka) sells a Chrome extension that fills the User ID and Password for a selected client on MCA V3 (and other portals); "You type the CAPTCHA, complete any OTP and click Sign in"; it "Never solves, bypasses or touches CAPTCHA", never automates OTP and never submits; it says it is "not affiliated with or endorsed by any government body"; ₹299 per year. — [RMK Tech Services, MCA login filler](https://rmktechservices.com/portal-login-filler/mca-login) (vendor's own claims)
- SAG Infotech says ROC tools "fetch real-time data via the MCA database" and "support in pre-filing the web-based forms with correct CIN-based data", and that users log in through business user roles and "fill forms online"; it refers to "MCA's new APIs" without naming them. — [SAG Infotech blog](https://blog.saginfotech.com/roc-software-handle-mca-ver-3-forms-filing); [Gen CompLaw product page](https://saginfotech.com/GenCompanye-filer.aspx) (marketing; the earlier research found no public MCA filing API, so the "API" claim is unverified)
- ComplyRelax says it pulls company and director details from records and the MCA database, auto-fetches DINs, and offers AI-based autofill from uploaded financial statements. — [ComplyRelax](https://www.complyrelax.com/) (marketing)
- V3 login sequence reported by guides: user ID and password, captcha, then OTP to registered mobile/email; DSC login also offered. — [IncorpX, V3 login errors 2026](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/mca-v3-login-errors-fixes-2026); [corplawupdates, MCA21 glossary](https://www.corplawupdates.in/glossary/mca21) (secondary)
- Business Standard (November 2026): persistent V3 glitches delay filings and incorporations; an ICSI representation lists downtime, slow responses, automatic logouts, pre-fill and retrieval errors and DSC problems. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/mca-portal-glitches-disrupt-corporate-operations-trigger-repeated-filing-126110601983_1.html); [Taxscan, ICSI urges extension](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/mca-v-portal-glitches-icsi-urges-mca-to-extend-filing-deadlines-waive-additional-fee-for-sept-dec-forms-1434949)
- No report of MCA blocking bots, RPA or extensions, or of a captcha/MFA change aimed at automation, was found in 2025 or 2026 searches. — [search summarised in this note; closest items: CAclubindia on June 2026 data-centre extension](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/mca-extends-name-reservation-and-e-form-resubmission-deadlines-till-july-10-2026-26608.asp)

### Inferences
- Open sale of these tools for years without MCA objection is evidence of tolerance, not permission. It supports the view that attended assistance is ordinary practice; it does not protect conduct that breaks the terms or the IT Act.
- VCFO Assist is more conservative than the RMK precedent: it does not touch credentials at all. The firm should not add a credential-fill feature, because storing client or director MCA passwords is the s.66C and "misuse of passwords" risk.
- V3's reported automatic logouts and pre-fill errors are an operational risk for an auto-fill tool: the extension must detect session loss and must not assume MCA's own pre-filled values are correct.

### Gaps
- Vendors' terms and actual product mechanics (in-browser fill versus offline preparation) were not verified by testing.
- No MCA statement on captcha or MFA for bots was found; mca.gov.in notices could not be searched directly.

## 6. Indian case law on browser automation or scraping of government portals

### Takeaway
No Indian judgment on browser automation, RPA or scraping of the MCA, GST or income-tax portals was found. The closest authority is the Supreme Court's January 2025 IRCTC ruling, which upheld prosecution of an unauthorised person running a ticket-procurement business through hundreds of fake user IDs, under a Railways Act offence that has no Companies Act counterpart. Courts so far protect government portals through sector statutes and the portals' own terms, not through a general anti-automation rule.

### Cited Findings
- Inspector, Railway Protection Force, Kottayam v. Mathew K. Cherian & Anr., 2025 INSC 51 (Datta and Mishra JJ., 9 January 2025): s.143 Railways Act (unauthorised carrying on of the business of procuring and supplying tickets) covers online e-ticket procurement, not just offline; a person who is "neither a railway servant nor an authorised agent" and allegedly "had created hundreds of fake user IDs to sell tickets" must face trial; proceedings against an authorised agent were treated differently, though authorised agents cannot do unauthorised acts "under the façade of authorisation". — [Supreme Court judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2017/6192/6192_2017_15_1501_58348_Judgement_09-Jan-2025.pdf); [LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/supreme-court/unauthorised-sale-of-railway-tickets-online-offence-supreme-court-restores-cases-for-fraudulent-irctc-site-use-280492); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/01/15/s143-railways-act-criminalise-multiple-user-id-irctc-sc-legal-news/)
- Reports differ on whether the Court held that creating multiple user IDs is itself an offence; one summary says the provision "is completely silent on creation of multiple user IDs". — [CaseMine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/in/new-legal-principle-on-section-143-of-the-railways-act:-distinction-between-authorized-and-unauthorized-agents/view); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/unauthorized-railway-e-ticket-declared-illegal)
- GST portal: the GSTN user agreement treats web scraping as an unauthorised s.43 activity and limits access to authorised interfaces (a contractual and policy position, not a judgment). — [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/scrapping-of-gst-portal-site-is-strictly-prohibited-gstn.html)
- Delhi HC (J M Jain v. Union of India) cautioned against AI-generated fake citations in GST show-cause notices; it concerns AI drafting by the department, not portal automation. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/issuing-scn-after-independent-scrutiny-of-income-tax-records-valid-cautions-against-ai-citations-delhi-hc-311398)

### Inferences
- The IRCTC ruling is not a bar on VCFO Assist: there is no Companies Act offence of "unauthorised filing business", professionals are expressly allowed to prepare and certify SPICe+, and VCFO Assist uses one genuine user ID per staff member. Its lesson is the risk line: fake or multiple IDs and getting around portal limits are what courts punish.
- The Delhi HC AI-citation caution supports keeping a human verification step over any AI-derived value.

### Gaps
- No Indian judgment on s.43 or s.66 applied to RPA or extensions on any government portal was found; Indian Kanoon was not searched exhaustively.

## 7. Classification of each VCFO Assist act

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, every act VCFO Assist performs is lawful when done attended, in the staff member's own session, at human pace, with data the certifier verifies. None is expressly permitted by MCA, so each is "Allowed with conditions" rather than "Allowed", and under SBC's own rule each still needs the firm's written CS sign-off before live use. The acts the design already excludes are the ones that would be prohibited.

### Cited Findings
- Basis for each row: MCA Website Policies (unauthorised use/entry, misuse of passwords, automated high-volume access) — [MCA, Website Policies](https://mcacdm.nic.in/docs/tnc.pdf); IT Act ss.2(1)(a), 43, 66, 66C, 70, 72A — [India Code](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/770a7d02-48f2-4274-bab9-10d0f48ee264/content); INC-32 certification and ICAI due-diligence orders — [ICAI DC/1890/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/85.-DC-1890-2024.pdf), [ICAI DC/1891/2024](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/125.-DC-1891-2024_compressed.pdf); one DSC per user ID and no OTP/password sharing — [MCA V3 FAQs](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca/pdf/v3FAQs_20022022.pdf), [ICSI V3 FAQ](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/FAQs_MCA-21_Login_Registration.pdf); GSTN contrast — [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/scrapping-of-gst-portal-site-is-strictly-prohibited-gstn.html); CCA token custody — [CCA FAQ](https://cca.gov.in/faq.html).

### Inferences

| # | Act | Classification | Reasons and conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Staff member logs in with own Business User ID, types captcha and OTP (no tool involvement) | Allowed | This is the registered user's own access; the tool does nothing. |
| 2 | Extension reads client data from the firm's own app and fills SPICe+ Part A (name reservation) fields in the user's own session | Allowed with conditions | Within the user's MCA permission (s.43 not engaged); no MCA clause bars input tools. Conditions: user present and triggering each fill; only the firm's clients; no placeholder or dummy values in mandatory fields (Gubiligari); proposed names and objects come from the client's instructions. |
| 3 | Extension fills SPICe+ Part B (incorporation, AGILE-PRO-S, e-MOA, e-AOA linked fields) | Allowed with conditions | Same as row 2. Extra condition: the certifier reviews the filled form field by field against originals or certified copies before any DSC is applied, and the review is logged (who, when, which version). Registered-office visit, Rule 13 witnessing and consents remain personal acts the tool cannot evidence. |
| 4 | Extension clicks Save draft and Check form | Allowed with conditions | User-equivalent actions on the user's own draft. Conditions: one click per user instruction, no automatic retry loops, back off on errors and session timeouts (V3 glitches), so it never approaches the "unusually high numbers of ... accesses ... in an automated fashion" clause. |
| 5 | Extension clicks Download PDF of the user's own form | Allowed with conditions | s.43(b) download is only an issue "without permission"; the user is entitled to their own form. Condition: only the current form, stored in the firm's system under its confidentiality controls. |
| 6 | Each signer signs the PDF with their own DSC token in emBridge | Allowed (and required) | Personal act under s.7(1)(b)/(c) and the CCA custody rule. Staff must never operate a signer's token or PIN. |
| 7 | Extension attaches a signed PDF the user selects | Allowed with conditions | Condition: the attached file must be the exact signed file (hash check against the version reviewed and signed), and the user confirms the attachment slot. Altering a document after signing would be a s.448 and due-diligence problem. |
| 8 | Human clicks final Submit and Pay | Allowed (and required) | Keeps the final statutory act and payment with the professional. |
| 9 | Extension reads the SRN and status from the screen into the firm's app | Allowed with conditions | Reading the user's own filing result is within permission. Condition: only the user's own filings, no crawling of other pages or company master data. |
| 10 | Extension fills login, captcha or OTP, or solves captcha | Prohibited (excluded by design) | Defeats an access control (strongest s.43 fact pattern); "misuse of passwords" in MCA terms; s.66C if another person's credentials. |
| 11 | Staff operates the extension inside a client's or director's MCA user ID, or shares one ID among staff | Prohibited | One user ID per person and no OTP/password sharing (MCA FAQs); s.66C and the terms' "misuse of passwords". The issue is the login, not the extension. |
| 12 | Extension or staff applies a signer's DSC | Prohibited (excluded by design) | CCA custody; s.66C; the certificate and declarations are personal statements. |
| 13 | Extension calls MCA endpoints directly or replays portal requests | Needs legal opinion, treat as prohibited (excluded by design) | Not used. MCA has no "authorised interface" clause, but direct calls look like system access outside the user interface and raise s.43 questions; no published MCA API. |
| 14 | Unattended, batched or high-rate runs (many forms or companies in a loop), or harvesting company/director search data | Prohibited under SBC's rules; at minimum Needs legal opinion | MCA terms allow denial of access for automated high-volume access; s.43(b)/(e) risk; loses the "attended" premise that carries rows 2 to 9. |
| 15 | Auto-filling firm-side contact details (firm email or phone) as the company's | Needs legal opinion | ICAI held a firm email in incorporation forms not guilty in DC/1891/2024, but fake phone numbers guilty; safer to fill the company's own details. |
| 16 | Values derived by AI or OCR from client documents filled without field-level human check | Needs legal opinion, treat as prohibited | Certifier must have "verified the above particulars"; Delhi HC caution on unchecked AI output; DPDP and confidentiality if client data goes to an outside model. |
| 17 | If MCA21 is ever notified as a s.70 protected system | Needs legal opinion at that time | Access then limited to persons authorised under s.70(2); up to 10 years for unauthorised access. Not notified as far as found. |

### Gaps
- The classifications rest on the 2018 Website Policies text from the NIC mirror. If the live V3 terms or SPICe+ page terms add an "authorised interface", "no automated tools" or "no browser extensions" clause, rows 2 to 9 would move to Needs legal opinion. A CS must read the live V3 terms from an Indian network before sign-off.
- No regulator, court or institute has spoken directly on attended browser auto-fill; the classifications are reasoned from general text, not from a ruling on this fact pattern.
