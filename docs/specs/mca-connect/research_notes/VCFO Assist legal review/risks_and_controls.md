# VCFO Assist: practical legal risks, enforcement record and controls (as of 10 November 2026)

Scope: an Indian CA/CS firm (SBC) whose staff-only Chrome extension, VCFO Assist, fills MCA V3 SPICe+ forms in the staff member's own logged-in session, plus an AI feature in VCFO Suite. The firm's own design (read first): `docs/specs/vcfo-assist-agents/PLAN.md`, `SAFETY.md` (guards G1 to G15: never fill login, captcha or OTP; never submit, pay or sign; no portal endpoint calls; no stored credentials; labels-only AI payload; AI picks only an index; live-portal switch held off until a written CS opinion; attended only; action-bound confirmation table; audit trail in `assist_runs`; signed recipes), `CS-QUESTIONS.md` (eleven Part A questions to the CS, including the MCA terms of use, s.70 "protected system", DSC custody, review evidence under s.7(6) and s.448, DPDP and offshore hosting, and the statute texts) and `docs/OVERNIGHT-REPORT.md` (what was built on 9 and 10 November 2026; nothing deployed).

Research note: this pass used about 23 search and fetch calls. Several primary documents (ICAI's own AI guidance note, the ICAI group PI policy wording, ICSI disciplinary orders on SPICe+) could not be retrieved, and these are listed under Gaps.

## 1. MCA/ROC and institute enforcement against professionals for wrong or false incorporation filings

### Takeaway
The realistic way a CA/CS gets stuck is not the software. It is a SPICe+ certification that says "I personally visited the registered office" or "subscribers signed in my presence" when that did not happen. ROCs file complaints with ICAI/ICSI. The usual result is a reprimand, sometimes a fine or removal from the register for a period. Professionals who can show real due diligence, or an IP or log trail proving misuse, are acquitted. The 2022 Chinese shell-company crackdown put about 400 CAs and CSs under scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
- **Chinese shell-company crackdown (2022).** The Central Government recommended disciplinary action against about 400 CAs and CSs for their alleged role in incorporating Chinese shell companies by flouting norms. Many of the companies were not found at their registered addresses. Certification by CSs and CAs is required at incorporation, which is why professionals were targeted. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/disciplinary-action-initiated-against-400-chartered-accountants-company-secretaries-for-incorporating-chinese-shell-companies/185225); [A2Z Taxcorp summary](https://a2ztaxcorp.com/400-chartered-accountants-company-secretaries-under-scanner-for-flouting-norms-to-incorporate-chinese-shell-companies/)
- Reports say about 200 CA firms received ICAI notices after ROC offices complained to ICAI's disciplinary directorate. Figures (400 professionals, 200 firms, 500 companies) differ between outlets. — [CA Rajput Jain](https://carajput.com/learn/icai-issue-notice-to-ca-firms-on-violate-company-law-via-chinese-co.html); [CASansaar](https://www.casansaar.com/news-MCA/MCA-explores-Disciplinary-Action-against-CA-CS-in-500-Chinese-Companies/13491.html)
- **SFIO arrest, September 2022.** The SFIO arrested Dortse, described as the mastermind who incorporated many shell companies with Chinese links and supplied dummy directors. The sources do not report an arrest of a CA or CS in this matter. — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/sfio-arrests-mastermind-behind-incorporating-shell-companies-with-chinese-links-430652)
- **ICAI v CA Rounak Kumar Bansal (M. No. 554881), DC/1720/2023, Bench IV.** The complainant was the Deputy ROC, NCT of Delhi & Haryana, over the SPICe+ certification of Karefin Technology Pvt Ltd. Inspectors found no name board at the address, no employee present, and a guard who had never heard of the company, which bore "typical characteristic of a Shell Company". The respondent said "it is not feasible for a professional to visit the registered office", yet in SPICe+ he had declared that he had personally visited the premises. He received the papers from another CA "on behalf of the promoters" and had no direct contact with the subscribers, although he had declared that the MOA and AOA were signed in his presence. The Prima Facie Opinion was dated 28 September 2022 and the findings 10 October 2024. He was found guilty under Item (7), Part I, Second Schedule (no due diligence) for a wrong declaration in breach of Rule 13 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. He was **reprimanded** under s.21B(3)(a) by order of 20 January 2025. — [ICAI DC order PDF](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/130.-DC-1720-2023.pdf)
- **ICAI v CA Puneet Jain (New Delhi).** The complainant was the Deputy ROC (Shri Alpesh Marliya) over INC-22 (registered office) for Pienance Information Technology Pvt Ltd. The CA argued that his signature and DSC had been forged, that he practised only in Delhi while the form was signed in Mumbai, and that he had filed a police complaint. The Committee held that responsibility for safekeeping a DSC lies with its owner under the IT Act, and that he had certified without the proof of ownership Rule 25 requires. He was found guilty under Item (7) and reprimanded under s.21B(3)(a). Findings 26 November 2024, hearing 6 January 2025, order 20 January 2025. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/ca-reprimanded-misuse-dsc-mca-filing.html)
- **Acquittals on due diligence.** CA Vivek Kalani was cleared of certifying a false registered office because he had done due diligence. In a ROC Mumbai complaint against CA Dhruvaprakash Shetty, the premises belonged to a director's relative who gave written consent, and the ROC inspected five years later; the complaint was dismissed. CA Abhishek Bindal was acquitted partly because the new address was close to the old one. — [TaxGuru (Kalani)](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/icai-disciplinary-committee-finds-ca-guilty-inc-22-certification-case.html); [StudyCafe (Shetty)](https://studycafe.in/icai-slams-5-year-late-roc-inspection-clears-ca-in-alleged-false-certification-case-397385.html); [TaxGuru (Bindal)](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/icai-acquits-ca-roc-misconduct-case-involving-delhi-based-companies.html)
- Other ICAI Bench-IV orders arising from ROC complaints describe dummy subscribers and directors with forged documents (for example DC-1716-2023, an INC-32 certification). — [ICAI DC-1716-2023](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/129.-DC-1716-2023.pdf)
- **ICSI sanctions.** ICSI's Disciplinary Committee can remove a member's name for a period and impose a fine. In one appeal the DC removed a name for one year with a fine, and the Appellate Authority reduced it. In another case, three Secretarial Compliance Certificates issued on a single day led to an order that was quashed and remanded because the member was not heard on the quantum of punishment. An older ICSI DC order exists in the matter MCA v Gautam Bandopadhyay (ACS-6048), dated 18 January 2016. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/icsi-aa-reduces-penalty-professional-misconduct-errant-member.html); [ABC Aus](https://abcaus.in/icsi/cs-misconduct-order-quashed-no-opportunity-heard-quantum-punishment.html); [ICSI order PDF](https://www.icsi.edu/media/website/ORDER_DC_239_MCA%20Vs%20Gautam%20Bandopadhyay,%20ACS-6048_18.01.2016.pdf)
- **s.448 and s.447.** Section 448 (false statement in any return, report or certificate) has no punishment of its own; the guilty person is "liable under Section 447" (fraud). A s.448 case is therefore effectively a s.447 offence, tried in court, not penalty-adjudicated by the ROC. — [Corporate Professionals guide](https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/ADJUDICATION-COMPOUNDING-AND-PROSECUTION-UNDER-THE-COMPANIES-ACT.pdf); see also [BCAJ corporate law corner](https://bcajonline.org/mail_category/corporate-law-corner/)
- **Incorrect e-form data, adjudicated.** ROC Kolkata, order dated 3 February 2026: an AOC-4 filed with incorrect data breached Rule 8(3) of the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, with a penalty of ₹10,000 each on the company and one director. This shows that ROCs penalise wrong data in e-forms even without fraud. — [ICSI CSJ "Gist of ROC Adjudication Orders", March 2026](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/Mar2026/34.pdf)

### Inferences
- Every guilty finding found here turns on a **false personal declaration in the certificate** (site visit, signatures in presence, proof of ownership). None turns on the software used to type the form. VCFO Assist's main legal risk is that it makes filings faster and more numerous while the certifier's due diligence stays the same, or gets weaker.
- The defence that worked (the 2023 Chennai DSC case, and Kalani and Shetty) was **contemporaneous evidence**: IP logs, a written consent from the premises owner, and records of verification. The `assist_runs` audit trail (G14) and the G13 confirmation table are therefore evidence assets, provided they also capture the certifier's own verification steps (site-visit photo and geotag, a KYC video call with subscribers, the NOC from the owner) and not just the fills.
- Penalty ladder, practically: ROC adjudication (₹ thousands to lakhs) → ICAI/ICSI complaint (reprimand, fine, or removal for months or years) → s.448/447 prosecution (criminal). The statute texts (s.447's imprisonment range and its fine of up to three times the amount involved) are from general knowledge and were not verified in this pass. `CS-QUESTIONS.md` Q11 already asks for them.

### Gaps
- No ICSI order specifically on SPICe+ certification of a shell company was found. ICSI's disciplinary orders page should be searched directly.
- The final outcomes of the 400-professional Chinese shell-company referrals (how many were found guilty, or removed) were not found.
- No s.447 conviction of a CA or CS for an incorporation certificate was found.

## 2. DSC or MCA login misuse by staff of CA/CS firms (s.66C, identity theft)

### Takeaway
ICAI holds the **DSC owner** responsible for safekeeping. "My DSC was misused" is not a defence unless the owner can prove it (a different IP, a prompt cyber complaint, a letter to the ROC). Criminal cases for forged DSCs on the MCA portal are prosecuted as cheating and forgery plus IT Act offences.

### Cited Findings
- CA Puneet Jain (order of 20 January 2025): "responsibility for safekeeping a digital signature lies with its owner" under the IT Act; he was reprimanded despite his forgery claim and police complaint. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/ca-reprimanded-misuse-dsc-mca-filing.html)
- Contrast case (reported 22 November 2023, respondent unnamed, ROC Chennai): a DSC was obtained in the CA's name using an email ID the directors controlled, and used for Forms 18 and 32 in 2012. The CA filed a National Cyber Crime complaint and wrote to the ROC. MCA's technical team found that the IP address of the filings differed from his, and he was found **not guilty**. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/icai-disciplinary-committee-clears-ca-from-professional-misconduct-for-alleged-connivance-in-incorporation-of-shell-companies-read-order/345145)
- Criminal precedent (about 2015): three people from Delhi and Pune were arrested for forging digital signatures to pose as a company's directors on the MCA website. They were booked under IPC and IT Act provisions for cheating and forgery. It was detected by the company's own CS on a routine ROC check. — [Corporate Law Reporter](https://corporatelawreporter.com/ca-cs-arrested-faking-dsc/)
- A Delhi High Court judgment pronounced on 18 December 2025 (CRL.M.M. 8175/2025) appeared in the DSC-misuse search. Its holding was not read in this pass. — [Delhi HC PDF](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/SVN25122025CRLMM81752025_171516.pdf)

### Inferences
- For SBC, the scenario to fear is that a staff member, or a compromised extension, uses a partner's or client's logged-in session or DSC token left in the office. Liability then lands on the DSC owner, so on the professional or the client director, not on the staff member. G1, G6, G8 and G12 directly reduce this. DSC tokens held by the firm (CS question 6) remain the biggest residual exposure.
- IT Act s.66C (identity theft: fraudulent use of another person's electronic signature, password or unique identification feature; general knowledge, up to 3 years and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh, not re-verified here) and s.66D (cheating by personation) would be the charges against an individual staff member. s.43 and s.66 cover unauthorised access.
- The defence ICAI accepted rested on **IP and log evidence**. SBC should keep, per filing, the staff member's identity, device and IP, time stamps, and the signer's own attestation. G14 should log the filer's Business User ID owner and the DSC holder separately.

### Gaps
- No reported Indian case was found in which a CA or CS **firm** was held vicariously liable for an employee's misuse of a DSC or MCA login. There is also no case on s.85 IT Act (offences by companies) applied to a professional firm.

## 3. Data-breach and confidentiality actions; CERT-In; DPDP Board

### Takeaway
No Data Protection Board penalty order exists as of November 2026. The Board's provisions are in force, but the substantive duties (notice, security safeguards, breach notification) apply from about 13 May 2027. CERT-In's six-hour incident reporting and 180-day log retention apply now. No named enforcement against an Indian CA firm for a breach was found, but I4C has warned specifically about ransomware against CA firms.

### Cited Findings
- The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025 (some firms say 13 November). Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 (definitions and the Board) took effect at once. Rules 3 and 5 to 16, 22 and 23 (notice, security, breach notification and more) apply 18 months later, about 13 May 2027 (sources say 12, 13 or 14 May). For Rule 4 (consent managers), sources conflict between 13 November 2026 and a date still to be notified. — [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/); [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87799)
- Board status: one 2026 status note says the Board was established on 13 November 2025 but was still being staffed in mid-October 2026, and could not yet hold proceedings. No penalty orders were found. The penalty ceiling is ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, and appeals lie to TDSAT within 60 days. These are secondary sources of mixed quality. — [Dapro (status 2026)](https://dapro.in/blog/data-protection-board-status-2026); [Governance Docs](https://governancedocs.com/dpdp-penalties/)
- CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 (s.70B(6) IT Act), in force from 27 June 2022: service providers, intermediaries, data centres, **body corporates** and government bodies must report listed incidents within **6 hours** of noticing them, and keep logs of all ICT systems for a rolling **180 days**. The FAQs of 18 May 2022 allow logs abroad if a copy stays in India. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/security/1205718/de-coding-the-cert-in-directions-on-cyber-security); [Nishith Desai on the FAQs](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/cert-in-releases-faqs-explaining-the-direction-on-cybersecurity-6139); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/cert-in-directions/)
- I4C (the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre) warned of a surge of ransomware against CA and consulting firms. A March 2026 alert flags internet-exposed NAS devices at CA firms. — [TaxGuru-hosted alert PDF](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Cyber-Alert-NAS-Devices-Used-by-CA-Firms-at-Risk-of-Ransomware-Due-to-Internet-Exposure.pdf); [The420](https://the420.in/?p=68767)
- ICAI's own website faced an alleged SQL-dump breach posted on dark-web forums around 16 February 2026 (unconfirmed). ICAI denied a separate breach claim about its examiners' portal. — [UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/news/the-institute-of-chartered-accountants-of-india-data-breach-2026-02-17); [Careers360](https://finance.careers360.com/articles/icai-says-no-data-breach-despite-viral-website-message-and-social-media-claims)
- An unnamed large Indian professional-services firm reportedly had a breach in its GST software after a developer copied code with a backdoor from a public source. There is no date or primary source; treat it as anecdotal. — [CAalley](https://caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/ey-data-breach-puts-spotlight-on-data-security-at-big-four-as-clients-demand-tighter-controls)

### Inferences
- Until May 2027, SBC's live exposure on data is (a) professional confidentiality under the ICAI and ICSI Codes, (b) contract claims from clients, (c) CERT-In reporting and logs, and (d) s.43A and s.72A IT Act. The status of s.43A after DPDP's repeal provisions should be confirmed with counsel. From May 2027, SBC as data fiduciary needs a s.8(2) contract with every processor (the AI host, AWS) and breach notice to the Board and to data principals.
- The "copied code with a backdoor" anecdote maps directly to extension supply chain (section 5): dependency pinning and review matter.

### Gaps
- There are no reported court or regulator penalties against an Indian CA or CS firm for a client-data breach.
- The exact Rule 4 commencement and the Board's staffing status were not confirmed from the Gazette or MeitY.
- It is unconfirmed whether CERT-In has ever penalised a private body corporate under s.70B(7) for late reporting; none was found.

## 4. AI-specific professional guidance (ICAI, ICSI, global)

### Takeaway
ICAI has an ethical-AI guidance note (its text was not retrieved) and is building its own model with Sarvam AI, partly over confidentiality worries about public tools. ICSI has journal articles, not binding guidance. ICAEW's position is blunt: putting client data into public AI tools "will likely breach confidentiality". ### Cited Findings

This is per secondary sources only. — [Suvit summary](https://www.suvit.io/post/icai-ai-guidelines-for-indian-cas); [ICAI AI portal article](https://ai.icai.org/articles_details.php?id=269)
- ICAI and Sarvam AI plan a dedicated AI model for CAs. Commentary notes that uploading client data to public AI platforms raises confidentiality and data-protection concerns. — [CA Rajput Jain](https://carajput.com/learn/icai-and-sarvam-ai-to-develop-a-dedicated-ai-model-for-ca.html)
- ICSI: the *Chartered Secretary* themed its October 2025 issue "Artificial Intelligence: Reshaping Governance Fundamentally". An August 2026 article covers AI governance for CSs and a June 2026 one covers technology for practising CSs. No ICSI circular on AI and confidentiality was found. — [CSJ Oct 2025](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/October_2025/16.pdf); [CSJ Aug 2026](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/Aug2026/15.pdf); [CSJ June 2026](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/June2026/17.pdf)

- CCAB (the UK bodies' consultative committee): "Draft Statement to the Profession on Use of AI", exposure draft dated 6 June 2026. ICAEW also announced in 2026 that professional bodies had issued AI advice to members; the body of that release was not readable. — [CCAB ED PDF](https://www.ccab.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/CCAB.Draft-Statement-to-the-Profession-on-Use-of-AI.-Exposure-Draft.6.6.26-002.pdf); [ICAEW news 2026](https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2026-news-releases/professional-bodies-issue-ai-advice-to-members)

### Inferences
- SBC's G9 (labels only, values never leave the browser), the `mask.ts` second net and G10 (the AI returns only an index) go further than any professional-body guidance found, which mostly says "don't paste client data into public tools; get a DPA". The residual issue is the separate Suite AI feature (Ask VCFO). If it sends client data to an offshore model host, it needs a processor contract with no training on inputs and defined retention, plus disclosure in the engagement letter.

## 5. Chrome extension risks: store policy, distribution and supply chain

### Takeaway
The biggest real-world extension risk is a **hijacked developer account pushing a malicious auto-update**. In the Cyberhaven case of December 2024, the poisoned update stole cookies and session tokens, and at least 29 extensions were hit in the wider campaign. For a staff-only tool, self-hosting with enterprise force-install (and a pinned update URL), or a private domain-restricted listing, avoids public-store exposure. Chrome Web Store Limited Use rules apply if the extension is ever listed.

### Cited Findings
- **Cyberhaven, 24 to 25 December 2024.** A phished admin account published malicious version 24.10.4 to the Chrome Web Store. It exfiltrated cookies and authenticated sessions to an attacker domain and was live for about 24 to 25 hours. Only auto-updated users were hit. Clean version 24.10.5 followed, and customers were told to rotate credentials. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/27/cyberhaven-says-it-was-hacked-to-publish-a-malicious-update-to-its-chrome-extension); [SecurityWeek: campaign hit at least 29 extensions](https://www.securityweek.com/cyberhaven-chrome-extension-hack-linked-to-widening-supply-chain-campaign/)
- Further hijacks were reported in 2026 (for example "hackers hijacked 16 Chrome extensions", September 2026, and VPN extensions compromised in a coordinated attack). — [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/hackers-hijacked-16-chrome-extensions/); [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/multiple-chrome-vpn-extensions-compromised-in-coordinated-attack/)
- **Chrome Web Store Limited Use policy:** data use is limited to the disclosed single purpose. Transfers are allowed only to provide that purpose, to comply with law, to fight abuse, or in a merger with consent. Humans may read user data only with explicit consent, for security or for legal compliance. Selling or transferring data for advertising, to data brokers, or for creditworthiness or lending is banned. An affirmative compliance statement is required on the website. — [Chrome developer docs, Limited Use](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/limited-use)
- **Enterprise self-hosting:** `ExtensionInstallForcelist` plus `ExtensionSettings`. For later updates Chrome uses the manifest's update URL unless `override_update_url: true` is set, in which case the policy's URL is used. Keep the same CRX signing key across versions, or Chrome treats a build as a different extension. Serve update.xml over HTTPS. — [Google: Configure ExtensionSettings policy](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/9867568?hl=en); [Microsoft Edge self-host update](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/troubleshoot/microsoft-edge/development/self-host-extension-update); [Chromium extensions group](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/chromium-extensions/c/Bd8Agwkt0G8)

### Inferences
- Controls that mitigate the Cyberhaven pattern: (1) do not publish publicly; self-host or use a private, domain-restricted listing; (2) hardware-key MFA on the Google developer account and on whoever holds the CRX signing key; (3) set `override_update_url` and pin it to a firm-controlled host; (4) keep G15's signed recipes, but note that they do not protect against a malicious *extension* update; release signing and two-person release approval are needed too; (5) lock dependencies and review them (the GST-backdoor anecdote); (6) restrict host permissions to Suite plus the MCA hosts, as SAFETY.md already enforces with a build test; (7) the absence of stored cookies or tokens (G8) limits what a hijacked build can take, but a hijacked build running in a live MCA session could still read the page and act. So force-install should be limited to a named staff organisational unit, and a kill switch should exist (removing the extension from the force-install list).
- Background knowledge, not re-verified in this pass: Manifest V3 bans remotely hosted code, which supports the design of data-only signed recipes. Chrome on Windows and macOS blocks off-store installs unless they come from enterprise policy.

### Gaps
- The Chrome Web Store "private" (Workspace domain-only) and "unlisted" options, and the review requirements for extensions handling financial or authentication data, were not fetched from the primary policy pages.
- No India-specific incident of a malicious extension against MCA or GST portal users was found.

## 6. Engagement-letter and privacy-notice clauses for software, AI and cross-border subprocessors

### Takeaway
No Indian (ICAI or ICSI) template was found. The pattern in UK and US guidance: a short engagement-letter clause (AI and software may be used, the firm stays responsible, confidentiality applies), a separate client notice plus a tool and subprocessor register "available on request", treat AI vendors as subprocessors under contract, and give a right to object.

### Cited Findings
- Recommended structure: a short clause in the letter covering three points (AI may assist; the firm and named staff stay responsible; the same confidentiality applies), with a fuller notice and tool register outside the letter. Sample wording: "we may use artificial intelligence tools to assist with drafting, summarising and analysis. Our current list of tools and a short notice describing their use are available on request." — [Sagnik Bhattacharya (practitioner blog)](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/chartered-accountants-ai-client-disclosure); [ICAEW AI guidance for small practices commentary](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/accountancy-practice-icaew-ai-guidance)
- An AI vendor that touches client data is a subprocessor. It goes on the list, under an agreement, and clients are told; the model provider behind a product should also be listed. — [AI Lab template (secondary)](https://ai-lab-library.pages.dev/templates/security-compliance-starter-kit/ENGAGEMENT-LETTER-AI-CLAUSE)
- ACCA (UK, September 2026): engagement letters should accurately reflect data protection and location, third-party software and AI use, without becoming longer. — [ACCA In Practice, Sept 2026](https://www.accaglobal.com/caribbean/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2026/september/Engagement-letters-AI-and-PCRT.html)
- ICAEW engagement-letter helpsheets (TAS) exist as the UK baseline. — [ICAEW engagement letters](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/practice/engagement-letters)
- An Indian law-firm view (King Stubb & Kasiva) on DPDP compliance for tax and accounting firms covers cloud risks and professional confidentiality. — [KSK](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-compliance-for-tax-and-accounting-firms/)

### Inferences
Suggested clause set for SBC, to be drafted by counsel:
- (a) **Technology-assisted preparation.** Forms may be pre-filled by firm software from client-supplied data. Every filing is reviewed and signed by the named professional and the client's own signatories. The firm never holds or uses the client's passwords, OTPs or DSC PIN.
- (b) **Client duties.** The client must keep its DSC and OTP device under its own control. The client warrants that the data it supplies is true, and must confirm the registered office and subscriber identity personally (a video KYC or site-visit evidence).
- (c) **AI.** Which features use AI. Client personal data is not sent to AI models except as stated. There is no training on client data. Masking is applied.
- (d) **Subprocessors and location.** A list (hosting, email, WhatsApp, the AI host), with countries, kept as an annex or URL, with notice of changes.
- (e) **Breach notice** within a set number of hours, aligned with CERT-In's 6 hours and, from May 2027, DPDP.
- (f) **Limitation of liability**, kept within what the ICAI and ICSI codes and PI insurance allow.
- (g) A **DPDP notice** for directors and subscribers whose data the firm processes, ready for May 2027.

### Gaps
- No ICAI, ICSI or Indian law-firm published clause specifically on AI or automated MCA filing was found.

## 7. Insurance: professional indemnity and cyber

### Takeaway
ICAI offers members a group PI (errors and omissions) policy with New India Assurance. Its exclusion wording for cyber, data breach, fines and fraud could not be retrieved. In the UK, "silent cyber" is a live regulatory issue and cyber cover is sold separately. The likely gap for SBC is that PI does not cover regulatory fines, criminal or fraud findings, or a data breach. That points to a standalone cyber policy and written confirmation from the insurer about tech-assisted filing.

### Cited Findings
- The ICAI group scheme is "Professional Indemnity Errors & Omissions Insurance for Chartered Accountants" by New India Assurance, at discounted rates. — [ICAI CMP benefits](https://cmpbenefits.icai.org/professional-Indemnity-Insurance); [PI flyer](https://cmpbenefits.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Professional-Indemnity-flyer-1.pdf)
- ICAEW has consulted on its minimum approved PI wording, with "silent cyber" a focus for regulators and insurers. A separate ICAEW member cyber product exists (Marsh). — [ICAEW consultation](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/professional-indemnity-insurance/consultation-changes-to-minimum-approved-policy-wording); [Marsh ICAEW cyber](https://www.marshcommercial.co.uk/for-business/icaew/icaew-cyber)
- Indian broker pages describe CA PI as covering liability from errors, omissions and negligence, sometimes with a "loss of documents or data" extension. These are generic, not the ICAI policy. — [ICICI Lombard](https://sme.icicilombard.com/blogs/understanding-chartered-accountants-indemnity-insurance); [BimaKavach](https://www.bimakavach.com/blog/professional-indemnity-insurance-for-chartered-accountants-ca-everything-you-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- Ask the insurer in writing: (1) Are claims from filings prepared with firm-built automation or AI covered, or excluded as "technology services"? (2) Are acts of employees covered, including dishonest acts (fidelity)? (3) Are defence costs for ICAI or ICSI disciplinary and ROC proceedings covered, given that fines and penalties are usually uninsurable? (4) Are cyber, data breach and CERT-In or DPDP response costs covered, or excluded so that a cyber policy is needed? (5) The retroactive date, and claims-made notification rules. (6) Does the policy cover the firm and also the CS (ICSI) members, who are not covered by the ICAI scheme?

### Gaps
- The New India Assurance ICAI policy terms PDF returned 404 at its redirected URL, so its exclusions are unverified.
- No ICSI group PI scheme details were found.
